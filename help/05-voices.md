# Voices and reading aloud

**View → Read Aloud** (`Ctrl+Shift+U`) reads the document to you and marks each sentence as it goes. A bar in the window has play, skip by section or paragraph, speed and voice. Drag it anywhere, by its handle or any empty part of it. Double-click it to put it back at the bottom.

[Start reading aloud](#do=read-aloud)

## Changing the voice

Open [Preferences → Reading Aloud](#do=preferences:voices), or use the **Voice** list in the bar while reading. You can choose:

- **This computer's voices**, which are already installed. They can sound robotic.
- **Natural voices**: ten voices that sound like a person. Clara, Maya, Nora, Sofia and Zoe are female, and Ethan, Leo, Marcus, Owen and Theo are male.

## Getting the natural voices

1. Open the **Voice** list.
2. Choose **Get natural voices…** at the top.
3. Agree to the download, about 400 MB, one time only.
4. Pick a voice.

Every sentence is made on your computer, so nothing you read is sent anywhere, and they work without internet. They read 31 languages. To remove them, choose **Remove natural voices…** at the end of the same list.

## Making the natural voices sound the way you like

Open [Preferences → Reading Aloud](#do=preferences:voices). These four settings are for the natural voices, and they're used when reading aloud and when exporting audio. Changing one while it's reading carries on in the new way from the sentence you're on.

- **Natural voice pace** is how quickly a natural voice speaks: from 0.95× (slower) to 1.35× (quicker), with 1.15× as the starting point. The **Speed** above it then makes the voice faster or slower still, so 1.15× at a Speed of 1× is the pace you've chosen.
- **Quality** is how much work goes into each sentence. **Faster** is the quickest to make. **Balanced** is a little richer, and **Best** is the richest, and takes about twice as long as Faster. A computer that can't make speech as fast as it's read steps down by itself, one level at a time, and goes back up if it catches up. An audio export keeps the level you chose, so Best makes a long book take about twice as long as Faster.
- **Warmth** is a gentle tone filter: a little more body in the voice, a softer top, and the quiet and loud words brought a little closer together, at the same loudness. It runs from 0.90 to 1.20, with 1.05 as the starting point, and **Off** turns it off. At 1.00 the low voice is 3.5 dB fuller, the sound above 7 kHz is 3.5 dB softer, and there's a little extra presence around 450 Hz and 3 kHz.
- **Breathing** lets the voice take a breath before it begins and sigh when it ends, like a person reading to you. **At the start and end of a reading** gives one breath before the first sentence of the text, or the sentence you start from, and a sigh after the last sentence. **At each paragraph** gives a breath and a sigh around every paragraph, and never around a heading. It's off to begin with.

A dash in the text (—) is read as a pause, a little like a comma. A range such as 1990–2000 and a word such as well-known are read as they're written.

## Choosing your speakers

Open [Preferences → Reading Aloud](#do=preferences:voices) and pick a device under **Play through**. This works with the natural voices. The computer plays its own voices, so they always use its default device.

## Reading through slides

In a presentation, press **R**. Reading marks each sentence and turns the slides by itself. Press the arrow keys to move to another slide and the reading comes with you. Press **Esc** to leave the slides, and it carries on in the document.

## Teaching it how to say a word

[Open the pronunciation list](#do=pronunciation), type the word, then spell it the way it sounds. Press **Listen** to try it. Choose **All documents**, or just the documents in this folder and the folders inside it. It's used for reading aloud and for audio exports.
